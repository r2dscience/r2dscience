Asymmetry

The directional relation remaining after retention

R2D separates two enclosing contributions to local realization.

Possibility supplies directional support:

\[ \Delta P. \]

Retention opposes or retains that directional possibility across a readable distinction:

\[ \Delta R. \]

Their difference is local asymmetry:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A = \Delta P - \Delta R. } \]

Asymmetry is therefore not an independent third quantity added to possibility and retention.

It is what remains after they are compared.

Possibility favors.
Retention opposes.
Asymmetry is the directional relation that remains.

Constraint and Retention →

The Local Asymmetry of a Transition

Consider a readable transition at boundary \(B_n\):

\[ i \rightarrow k. \]

The enclosing boundary supplies the directional possibility

\[ \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

Productive constraint acting across the distinguishable interval supplies retention

\[ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

The net local asymmetry is therefore

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

or

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

This is the primitive directional relation at the local boundary.

Asymmetry Is Dimensionless

Part I has not yet introduced energy, force, temperature, chemical potential, or physical work.

Therefore

\[ \Delta A \]

is not primitively any of those quantities.

It is a dimensionless logarithmic count relation.

Later physical theories can assign energetic, mechanical, chemical, or other coordinates to it.

But the primitive definition remains

\[ \boxed{ \text{asymmetry} = \text{directional possibility} - \text{retention}. } \]

Asymmetry Is Not Multiplicity

The native multiplicity difference is

\[ \Delta S_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \ln \left( \frac{W_{n,k}}{W_{n,i}} \right). \]

This compares the possible local realization counts of two macrostates.

Asymmetry is different.

It depends on the enclosing compatibility and constraint relations under which the local transition is realized.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta S \neq \Delta A. } \]

The native multiplicity landscape can remain completely unchanged while \(\Delta A\) changes.

This distinction allows R2D to separate:

the structure of the local state space

from

the directional relation under which that state space is realized.

Multiplicity →

Asymmetry Is Not Occurrence

Occurrence count describes realized events.

For two macrostates,

\[ \Delta\tau_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \ln \left( \frac{\nu_{n,k}}{\nu_{n,i}} \right). \]

Under proportional occurrence realization,

\[ \Delta\tau \approx \Delta S. \]

Asymmetry does not replace this relation.

R2D instead gives asymmetry a different role.

It determines how the occurrence relation is expressed as occupancy.

This is one of the most important separations in Part I:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta S \longrightarrow \text{relative occurrence structure} } \]

while

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A \longrightarrow \text{occupancy bias}. } \]

Or more compactly:

Multiplicity structures occurrence.
Asymmetry structures occupancy.

Occupancy and Occurrence →

Asymmetry Biases Occupancy

Let

\[ G_{n,i} = \ln c_{n,i} \]

be the logarithmic occupancy coordinate.

R2D postulates

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta\tau_{n,i\rightarrow k} + \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

Substituting

\[ \Delta A = \Delta P-\Delta R \]

gives

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G = \Delta\tau + \Delta P - \Delta R. } \]

Under proportional occurrence realization,

\[ \Delta\tau \approx \Delta S, \]

so

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G \approx \Delta S + \Delta A. } \]

or equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G \approx \Delta S + \Delta P - \Delta R. } \]

This equation separates the two structures controlling realized occupancy.

The native multiplicity landscape supplies the relative occurrence structure.

The enclosing relation supplies the directional bias.

Asymmetry Does Not Create Occurrences

This distinction matters.

R2D does not say that asymmetry manufactures subordinate events.

The subordinate realization mechanism still supplies the occurrences.

Instead, asymmetry changes how those occurrences are expressed as occupancy.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{occurrence} \neq \text{occupancy bias}. } \]

A system can continue undergoing many subordinate transitions while its enclosing asymmetry changes the larger realized state distribution.

This is especially important in systems such as muscle, chemical reactions, and molecular ensembles.

Three Directional Regimes

The sign of

\[ \Delta A = \Delta P-\Delta R \]

defines three regimes relative to the chosen orientation

\[ i\rightarrow k. \]

Positive asymmetry

If

\[ \Delta P>\Delta R, \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A>0. } \]

The enclosing relation provides residual directional support toward \(k\).

This does not mean that every individual occurrence must proceed in that direction.

It means that the realized occupancy relation is biased in that orientation.

Stall

If

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P=\Delta R, } \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A=0. } \]

Directional possibility is completely retained.

This is the R2D stall condition.

Negative asymmetry

If

\[ \Delta R>\Delta P, \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A<0. } \]

The net occupancy bias lies in the opposite orientation.

So the sign of asymmetry is relational.

It depends on both the selected transition orientation and the enclosing conditions under which that transition is realized.

Stall Does Not Mean No Possibility

At stall,

\[ \Delta A=0. \]

But this does not require

\[ \Delta P=0. \]

Instead,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P=\Delta R. } \]

Possibility can remain finite.

It is simply completely retained.

This distinction becomes important in thermodynamic and mechanical systems.

A stalled molecular motor or muscle need not lack chemical possibility.

The possibility can remain present while mechanical retention matches it.

Thus:

Stall is not absence of possibility.
Stall is complete retention of possibility.

Zero Retention Is the Opposite Limit

If

\[ \Delta R=0, \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A=\Delta P. } \]

All of the directional possibility remains available as local asymmetry.

This is therefore very different from stall.

At zero retention:

\[ \Delta A=\Delta P. \]

At stall:

\[ \Delta A=0. \]

These two limits can produce similar-looking macroscopic observations in some physical systems—for example, zero productive output—but their local R2D meanings are opposite.

Asymmetry Can Change While Multiplicity Does Not

Suppose the native multiplicities remain fixed:

\[ W_{n,i}, \qquad W_{n,k}. \]

Then

\[ \Delta S \]

is fixed.

But the enclosing compatibility relation can change, altering

\[ \Delta P. \]

Or the enclosing productive constraint can change, altering

\[ \Delta R. \]

Therefore

\[ \Delta A \]

can change while the native multiplicity landscape does not.

Schematically:

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal{W}_n = \text{constant} } \]

while

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A \rightarrow \Delta A'. } \]

The occupancy landscape can consequently move through a fixed multiplicity landscape.

This is one of the central distinctions of R2D.

A Simple Example

Suppose a local transition has directional possibility

\[ \Delta P=5. \]

With no productive retention,

\[ \Delta R=0, \]

so

\[ \Delta A=5. \]

Now increase the enclosing constraint so that

\[ \Delta R=2. \]

Then

\[ \Delta A=3. \]

Increase it further:

\[ \Delta R=5. \]

Now

\[ \Delta A=0. \]

The system is at stall.

If retention increases again,

\[ \Delta R=7, \]

then

\[ \Delta A=-2. \]

The directional bias has reversed.

Nothing in this sequence requires the native multiplicities

\[ W_{n,i} \qquad\text{and}\qquad W_{n,k} \]

to change.

Only the enclosing constraint relation changed.

An Occupancy Example

Suppose under proportional occurrence realization,

\[ \Delta\tau \approx \Delta S = 2. \]

With no additional asymmetry,

\[ \Delta A=0, \]

the occupancy difference is

\[ \Delta G\approx2. \]

Now suppose the enclosing relation supplies

\[ \Delta A=3. \]

Then

\[ \Delta G\approx5. \]

The native multiplicity structure has not changed.

The occurrence relation still reflects that multiplicity.

But occupancy is shifted by the enclosing asymmetry.

This is precisely the distinction R2D is designed to preserve.

Equal Occupancy Does Not Require Equal Multiplicity

Suppose two competing structural macrostates reach an occupancy-exchange threshold:

\[ \Delta G\approx0. \]

Under proportional occurrence realization,

\[ \Delta G \approx \Delta S+\Delta A. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A \approx -\Delta S. } \]

The states can have unequal native multiplicities and still be equally occupied because enclosing asymmetry offsets the multiplicity difference.

This is the primitive count architecture that later acquires familiar thermodynamic forms.

But at this stage there is still no Gibbs free energy, temperature, or chemical potential.

There are only count relations.

Asymmetry Is Boundary Defined

One might try to locate asymmetry intrinsically inside a subordinate microstate transition.

R2D does not do this.

A transition that has microstate meaning relative to \(B_n\) is not directly readable there as an individual local transition.

To assign an asymmetry to it, one must move to the subordinate boundary at which that distinction has macrostate meaning.

But then its possibility and retention are defined relative to that boundary’s enclosing relation.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{asymmetry cannot be pushed downward without moving the boundary}. } \]

This is another expression of boundary-relative causality.

Macrostate and Microstate →

The Causal Direction

The occupancy relation

\[ \Delta G = \Delta\tau+\Delta A \]

does not mean occupancy creates asymmetry.

The proposed causal order is

\[ \boxed{ \text{enclosing compatibility and constraint} \Longrightarrow \Delta A \Longrightarrow \text{local occupancy realization}. } \]

In expanded form:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{enclosing compatibility} \rightarrow \Delta P \\[4pt] \text{enclosing constraint} \times \text{local distinguishability} \rightarrow \Delta R \\[4pt] \Delta P-\Delta R \rightarrow \Delta A \\[4pt] \Delta A \rightarrow \text{occupancy bias} \end{array} } \]

The subordinate transitions realize the resulting occupancy change.

They do not independently define the asymmetry under which they occur.

The Causal Inversion →

Local Return Does Not Require Asymmetry to Close

Asymmetry becomes even more interesting when a local state completes a return:

\[ i\rightarrow k\rightarrow i. \]

The native multiplicity difference around that completed path closes exactly:

\[ \Delta S_{\mathcal C}=0. \]

But the forward and return legs can occur under different enclosing compatibility and constraint relations.

Therefore their possibility and retention terms need not be exact reciprocals.

The accumulated closure asymmetry can satisfy

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C} = \Delta P_{\mathcal C} - \Delta R_{\mathcal C} \neq0 } \]

even though the local state has returned.

This is the primitive distinction between:

local state closure

and

closure of the enclosing directional relation.

They are not the same condition.

Closure →

Complete Retention Around a Closure

For a completed local closure,

\[ \mathcal C_{n,\alpha}, \]

possibility and retention accumulate separately:

\[ \Delta P_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} = \sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}} \Delta P_{n,\alpha,\chi}, \]

and

\[ \Delta R_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} = \sum_{\chi\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}} \Delta R_{n,\alpha,\chi}. \]

The net closure asymmetry is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} = \Delta P_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} - \Delta R_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C}. } \]

Complete retention around the closure satisfies

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C}=0, } \]

or equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} = \Delta R_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C}. } \]

Again, local macrostate return by itself does not require this condition.

Asymmetry Is What the Local Boundary Reads

The possibility relation originates in compatible enclosing multiplicity.

Retention originates in enclosing constraint acting across local distinguishability.

Their difference is readable locally as asymmetry.

Thus asymmetry is the local directional expression of a relation whose definition is not confined to the local boundary.

This is why R2D separates:

\[ \boxed{ \text{where a relation is realized} } \]

from

\[ \boxed{ \text{where its organization is defined}. } \]

That distinction leads directly to the causal inversion.

Core Equations

Local asymmetry

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

Retention

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

Occupancy realization

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta\tau_{n,i\rightarrow k} + \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

Under proportional occurrence realization

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G \approx \Delta S+\Delta A } \]

or

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G \approx \Delta S+\Delta P-\Delta R. } \]

Stall

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P=\Delta R \iff \Delta A=0. } \]

Closure asymmetry

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C} = \Delta P_{\mathcal C} - \Delta R_{\mathcal C}. } \]

The Key Distinction

The simplest summary is:

Multiplicity defines the available local structure.

Possibility supplies enclosing directional support.

Retention opposes that support across a readable distinction.

Asymmetry is what remains and biases occupancy.

So the architecture has now become:

\[ \boxed{ \text{multiplicity} \rightarrow \text{occurrence structure} } \]

together with

\[ \boxed{ \text{enclosing possibility} - \text{retention} \rightarrow \text{asymmetry} \rightarrow \text{occupancy bias}. } \]

The next question is causal:

If the asymmetry is defined by an enclosing relation but realized through subordinate transitions, which side organizes and which side realizes?
