From Count to Physical Science

Physical quantities are boundary-specific readings of recursive count

From Count to Physical Science

R2D begins before physics.

Not because physics is unimportant.

Because before a physical quantity can be measured, the relation being measured must already be distinguishable.

Part I therefore defines a dimensionless architecture of:

  • boundaries;
  • states;
  • multiplicity;
  • occurrence;
  • occupancy;
  • possibility;
  • constraint;
  • asymmetry;
  • closure;
  • recurrence;
  • recursive replacement;
  • recursive carry;
  • and background.

Only afterward does R2D ask how familiar scientific quantities read that architecture.

This gives the basic ordering:

\[ \boxed{ \text{primitive count relation} \rightarrow \text{boundary-specific physical read} \rightarrow \text{measured scientific quantity}. } \]

The physical quantity is real.

But it enters after the count relation it reads.

Before Units →

The Mapping Problem

Suppose R2D defines a dimensionless relation

\[ X_n. \]

A physical theory may supply a measurable quantity

\[ X_n^{\mathrm{phys}}. \]

The central question is not whether the two use similar mathematics.

It is:

Does the physical quantity preserve the role played by the primitive count relation?

A valid mapping must preserve:

  • the boundary at which the quantity is defined;
  • the distinctions it measures;
  • its causal role;
  • its relation to adjacent boundaries;
  • and the equations in which it participates.

Thus a physical mapping is stronger than symbolic resemblance.

Mapping Is Not Identity

This distinction is fundamental.

If a physical quantity reads a primitive R2D relation, that does not make the primitive quantity identical to the physical quantity in every domain.

For example,

\[ \lambda_n \]

may receive a physical displacement read in one mechanical realization.

That does not imply

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_n = \text{length} } \]

universally.

Likewise,

\[ \rho_{n+1|n} \]

may acquire a physical rate interpretation in a suitable system.

That does not imply

\[ \boxed{ \rho = \text{frequency} } \]

by definition.

The safe form is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{primitive count relation} \xrightarrow{\text{physical read}} \text{domain-specific quantity}. } \]

The Primitive Definition Must Survive the Mapping

This is the most important methodological rule.

Suppose a physical mapping is successful.

The mapped physical quantity does not replace the primitive definition.

Instead, both levels remain visible.

For example:

\[ \boxed{ \text{primitive: } S=\ln W } \]

can acquire a thermodynamic read.

But the logarithmic count relation remains the primitive structure underneath that thermodynamic coordinate.

Likewise:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R = C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda } \]

may acquire a mechanical work-like read.

The physical interpretation does not redefine primitive retention.

The physical read adds dimensional meaning.
It does not rewrite the count architecture.

Physical Constants Belong to the Read

Dimensionless count and dimensional measurement generally require a calibration between them.

Physical constants can provide that calibration.

The familiar Boltzmann relation is the clearest example.

R2D begins with

\[ S = \ln W. \]

A thermodynamic read assigns the Boltzmann scale:

\[ \boxed{ S^{\mathrm{th}} = k_B \ln W. } \]

The count

\[ \ln W \]

comes first.

The constant

\[ k_B \]

converts that logarithmic count coordinate into a thermodynamic entropy coordinate.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{count} \rightarrow \text{physical calibration} \rightarrow \text{unit-bearing quantity}. } \]

Thermodynamics Is the Strongest Bridge

Thermodynamics is especially important in the R2D program because many parts of the recursive architecture remain jointly readable there.

A thermodynamic system can often expose simultaneously:

  • multiplicity;
  • occupancy;
  • directional bias;
  • constraint;
  • recurrence;
  • work;
  • heat;
  • and background production.

This makes thermodynamics the strongest current bridge between the dimensionless architecture of Part I and familiar physical science.

The program therefore does not begin by trying to map R2D directly onto the most inaccessible limits of quantum gravity.

It begins where enough of the primitive relation remains measurable at once.

Entropy Reads Multiplicity

The first mapping is the most familiar.

Primitive multiplicity is

\[ W. \]

Its logarithmic coordinate is

\[ S = \ln W. \]

A thermodynamic entropy read is

\[ \boxed{ S^{\mathrm{th}} = k_B S = k_B\ln W. } \]

R2D interprets this ordering literally:

\[ \boxed{ \text{multiplicity} \rightarrow \text{logarithmic count} \rightarrow \text{thermodynamic entropy}. } \]

Entropy therefore does not create multiplicity.

It physically reads it.

Multiplicity →

Gibbs Free Energy Reads Thermodynamic Direction

The thermodynamic mapping becomes more interesting when enclosing possibility is added.

For the fixed-temperature, fixed-pressure chemical realization developed in the Canon, the count relation becomes

\[ \boxed{ \Delta S+\Delta P = - \frac{\Delta g}{k_BT}. } \]

Here

\[ \Delta S+\Delta P \]

is the dimensionless compatible-count direction.

The physical quantity

\[ \Delta g \]

is its molecular Gibbs free-energy read under the specified thermodynamic conditions.

The important ordering is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{compatible count direction} \rightarrow \text{Gibbs energetic read}. } \]

Gibbs free energy is therefore not inserted as a primitive R2D quantity.

It is a physical coordinate assigned to a relation already defined in count space.

Gibbs Direction Comes Before Work Realization

This also preserves an important distinction.

The thermodynamic Gibbs relation describes available direction before productive retention is applied.

R2D local asymmetry is instead

\[ \Delta A = \Delta P-\Delta R. \]

So:

\[ \boxed{ \text{available thermodynamic direction} \neq \text{work already performed}. } \]

A state function can organize the available relation.

A work coordinate describes one constrained realization of that relation.

This is why free energy can bound possible work without being identical to a specific work trajectory.

Constraint Can Acquire a Mechanical Read

Primitive retention is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R_n = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_n. } \]

At the primitive level,

\[ C^{\mathrm{out}} \]

is not force and

\[ \lambda \]

is not displacement.

But in a mechanical realization, they may acquire those reads.

Schematically,

\[ C^{\mathrm{out}} \xrightarrow{\text{mechanical read}} F, \]

and

\[ \lambda \xrightarrow{\text{mechanical read}} d. \]

Then

\[ C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda \]

can acquire the familiar work-like form

\[ \boxed{ Fd. } \]

The mapping is powerful precisely because the primitive relation existed before force and distance were assigned.

Generalized Work Is More Fundamental Than One Mechanical Example

Nothing in

\[ \Delta R = C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda \]

requires the physical read to be force times linear displacement.

Different thermodynamic systems may map the same structural relation onto different conjugate variables.

Possible physical realizations include relations involving:

  • force and displacement;
  • pressure and volume;
  • electrical potential and charge;
  • chemical coupling;
  • elastic deformation;
  • or other generalized constraint-displacement pairs.

Thus:

R2D does not make one form of work primitive.

It makes the underlying constraint-distinguishability relation primitive.

Muscle Makes Several Reads Simultaneously Accessible

Muscle is especially useful because multiple parts of the recursive architecture can be measured within one functioning system.

At a molecular boundary, protein-state recurrence can be related to chemical cycling.

At an enclosing muscle boundary, force and shortening are mechanically readable.

Heat is measurable.

Occupancy of molecular states can be measured.

Chemical free-energy relations are experimentally accessible.

This makes muscle unusually capable of testing whether distinct physical coordinates actually occupy the structural roles assigned by R2D.

The muscle mapping is therefore more than an analogy.

It is an empirical calibration problem.

Physical Rate Is a Separate Mapping

Recurrence must be treated carefully.

Part I defines the adjacent recurrence ratio

\[ \rho_{n+1|n} = \frac{ N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_{n+1}) }{ N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_n) }. \]

This is a ratio of counts.

It is not yet a ratio of physical rates.

A physical system may provide measurable recurrence rates

\[ r_n \]

and

\[ r_{n+1}. \]

Only after that physical calibration has been justified can one test a mapping of the form

\[ \boxed{ \rho_{n+1|n} \xrightarrow{\text{rate read}} \frac{r_{n+1}}{r_n}. } \]

Thus physical time and rate do not enter by simply renaming recurrence.

They require an empirical bridge.

Recurrence →

State-Occurrence Count Is Not a Clock

This distinction also protects the meaning of

\[ \tau_{n,i} = \ln\nu_{n,i}. \]

The quantity

\[ \tau \]

is a logarithmic occurrence-count coordinate.

It is not primitive elapsed time.

It is not a physical rate.

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{ \tau \neq t. } \]

A physical clock may later read recurrence.

That does not turn every occurrence coordinate into time.

Background Can Acquire a Thermodynamic Entropy Read

The R2D Second Law gives

\[ \delta_{\mathcal C}\Phi_{n+1} = L_{\tau,n,\alpha,\mathcal C}. \]

Both are dimensionless logarithmic count relations.

In thermodynamics, the enclosing background increment can acquire an entropy read:

\[ \boxed{ S_{\mathrm{gen},\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{th}} = k_B \delta_{\mathcal C}\Phi_{n+1}. } \]

This provides the bridge from primitive occurrence nonreciprocity to thermodynamic entropy generation.

The logical order remains:

\[ \boxed{ \text{local occurrence nonreciprocity} \longleftrightarrow \text{enclosing background increment} \rightarrow \text{generated thermodynamic entropy}. } \]

The R²D Second Law →

Heat Is Not Automatically Entropy Production

This mapping also clarifies why familiar thermodynamic quantities must not be collapsed.

Heat can be exchanged reversibly.

Work can be performed reversibly.

Constraint can exist in a reversible system.

A temperature difference can exist without itself being entropy production.

The primitive irreversible coordinate is instead the nonreciprocity

\[ L_\tau>0. \]

Its enclosing thermodynamic read is generated entropy.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{heat} \neq \text{entropy production} } \]

in general.

The count architecture separates these relations before thermodynamic units are attached.

Probability Is a Normalized Read

Probability provides another example of physical or statistical reading.

Possible count exists first:

\[ W_{n,i}. \]

A normalized possible-count distribution can then be written

\[ \boxed{ p_{n,i} = \frac{ W_{n,i} }{ \sum_j W_{n,j} }. } \]

Likewise, realized occupancy can be normalized:

\[ \boxed{ p_{n,i}^{\mathrm{obs}} = \frac{ c_{n,i} }{ \sum_j c_{n,j} }. } \]

Normalization does not create the underlying count domain.

It reads it fractionally.

Thus:

Possible count precedes probability.

And:

Occupancy precedes normalized observed occupancy.

A Probability Read Must Specify What Was Normalized

This matters because several different R2D counts can be normalized.

One could normalize:

  • possible multiplicity;
  • occurrence count;
  • occupancy;
  • or another appropriate finite count.

Those normalized quantities need not have the same interpretation.

Therefore simply seeing a number between zero and one does not identify its primitive origin.

The count being normalized must first be specified.

Physical Geometry Is Also a Mapping

R2D defines a count boundary before assigning spatial geometry to it.

Likewise, it defines distinguishability

\[ \lambda \]

before interpreting that distinction as physical distance.

A spatial mapping may therefore take the schematic form

\[ \boxed{ \text{boundary} \xrightarrow{\text{spatial read}} \text{area or enclosing geometry} } \]

and

\[ \boxed{ \lambda \xrightarrow{\text{spatial read}} \text{distance-like coordinate}. } \]

The physical geometry can be completely real.

The R2D claim is only that the count relation is logically prior to that geometric representation.

Space Is Not Assumed in Order to Define a Boundary

This is why an R2D boundary is not initially a spatial surface.

The boundary defines a count domain.

Only later may a physical system represent that boundary geometrically.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ B_n \neq \text{primitive physical area}. } \]

But a particular physical mapping may assign an areal read to (B_n).

The distinction is exactly analogous to assigning an energetic read to a dimensionless logarithmic count coordinate.

Time Is Not Assumed in Order to Define Recurrence

Likewise, recurrence does not require an already-existing clock.

A completed relation can be counted again.

That count relation exists before seconds are assigned.

A physical clock then provides a temporal read of recurrence.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{recurrence} \rightarrow \text{clock read} } \]

rather than requiring

\[ \text{primitive time} \rightarrow \text{recurrence}. \]

This becomes especially important in the recursive-clock program.

Thermodynamics Occupies a Joint-Readability Regime

R2D later asks what happens when distinguishability and recurrence do not remain equally readable.

Thermodynamics is special because both can often be accessed together.

A thermodynamic experiment may simultaneously measure:

\[ \text{state distinction}, \]

\[ \text{occupancy}, \]

\[ \text{recurrence rate}, \]

\[ \text{constraint}, \]

and

\[ \text{background production}. \]

This makes thermodynamics the natural calibration regime for the primitive architecture.

Quantum Readability Emphasizes Recurrence

At one readability limit, subordinate geometric distinguishability becomes difficult or impossible to resolve while recurrence remains sharply readable.

The later R2D program asks whether familiar quantum quantities are physical projections appropriate to that regime.

Frequency, phase, occupancy statistics, amplitudes, and particle counts then become candidate reads of what remains accessible.

But the logical status must remain explicit:

The current Canon does not derive all of quantum mechanics from Part I.

The quantum program tests whether quantum formalism can be deprojected onto the same recursive count architecture.

Gravitational Readability Emphasizes Distinguishability

At the opposite readability limit, enclosing distinguishability can remain readable after the relevant enclosing recurrence is no longer jointly resolved.

The later gravitational program asks whether geometric quantities such as

  • distance;
  • area;
  • metric;
  • curvature;
  • and horizon structure

are physical reads appropriate to that limit.

Again, the distinction is essential:

A successful geometric mapping does not make geometry primitive to the count architecture.

Geometry can be real without being logically first.

The Two Physical Limits Need Not Use the Same Ruler

This offers a different view of the quantum-gravity problem.

If quantum physics and gravity occupy opposite readability limits of the same recursive relation, their most natural physical coordinates need not be the same.

One side may preserve recurrence more directly.

The other may preserve distinguishability more directly.

Trying to force both descriptions into one already-dimensional primitive ruler may therefore begin after the important distinction has already been lost.

R2D instead looks for the common structure before dimensional projection.

Unification Happens at the Count Level

The proposed unification is therefore not

\[ \boxed{ \text{quantum object} = \text{gravitational object}. } \]

Nor is it

\[ \boxed{ \text{quantum unit system} = \text{gravitational unit system}. } \]

It is

\[ \boxed{ \text{same primitive count grammar} \rightarrow \text{different boundary-specific physical reads}. } \]

This is unification before units.

Different Sciences Need Not Share the Same Objects

The same logic extends beyond physics.

A molecule is not a cell.

A cell is not an organism.

An organism is not an ecosystem.

Yet each can be represented through:

  • a count boundary;
  • readable states;
  • compatible subordinate realizations;
  • multiplicity;
  • occurrence;
  • occupancy;
  • enclosing compatibility;
  • constraint;
  • closure;
  • and recurrence.

Thus R2D does not unify the sciences by insisting that they study the same object.

It asks whether their different objects become scientifically defined through the same formal grammar.

Scientific Objecthood →

The Physical Read Can Change Without Changing the Primitive Role

Suppose

\[ C\lambda \]

has one mechanical read in muscle.

Another physical system might assign the same primitive role to a completely different conjugate pair.

The physical variables change.

The structural position in the recursive architecture does not.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{same primitive role} \not\Rightarrow \text{same physical quantity}. } \]

This is one of the central advantages of working dimensionlessly first.

Conversely, the Same Physical Quantity Can Play Different Roles

The reverse caution also matters.

The word “energy,” for example, appears in many contexts.

But two energetic quantities at different boundaries need not read the same primitive R2D relation.

Likewise, two quantities both called entropy may refer to different count relations.

Therefore matching physical units alone is not enough to establish an R2D mapping.

The structural role must also match.

Never Map by Units Alone

Suppose two quantities both have units of energy.

That does not prove that they correspond to the same count coordinate.

Likewise, two rates need not correspond to the same recurrence relation.

Two lengths need not correspond to the same distinguishability relation.

A valid mapping requires:

\[ \boxed{ \text{matching dimensions} + \text{matching boundary role} + \text{matching recursive relation}. } \]

Units are necessary for a physical calibration.

They are not sufficient to identify the primitive quantity.

Never Map by Equation Shape Alone

Another danger is recognizing a familiar equation and assigning R2D symbols to it afterward.

That reverses the method.

The correct order is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{define the R²D boundary and count relations first} } \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \text{identify independently measurable physical quantities} } \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \text{test whether the mapping preserves the relation}. } \]

A physical equation that merely resembles an R2D equation is not yet a derivation.

The Boundary Must Be Specified First

Every physical mapping should therefore begin by asking:

What is the scientific object?

Then:

What boundary defines it?

Only after that can we ask:

What are its readable macrostates?

What are its compatible subordinate realizations?

What counts as multiplicity?

What is occupancy?

What is the enclosing relation?

What closes?

What recurs?

Without a defined boundary, the physical dictionary is underdetermined.

The Physical Mapping Workflow

A disciplined R2D mapping proceeds in the following order:

  1. Define the object and count boundary.

  2. Identify its readable macrostates.

  3. Identify compatible subordinate realizations.

  4. Construct the native multiplicity structure.

  5. Separate multiplicity, occurrence, and occupancy.

  6. Identify enclosing compatibility and directional possibility.

  7. Identify constraint and retention.

  8. Define closure and recurrence.

  9. Write the dimensionless R2D relation.

  10. Only then identify candidate physical quantities that read those relations.

  11. Test the resulting mapping against established empirical law.

  12. State what was derived, what was mapped, and what remains hypothetical.

This order prevents a familiar physical ontology from being silently inserted into the primitive definitions.

Different Claims Have Different Logical Status

Not every equation in the R2D program has the same status.

This distinction should remain explicit.

Formal definition

A relation may follow directly from how the count domain is defined.

For example,

\[ W_{n,i} = \left| \pi_n^{-1}(i) \right|. \]

Mathematical consequence

A relation may follow from the definitions.

For example, native multiplicity closes around a genuine return:

\[ \Delta S_{\mathcal C}=0. \]

R2D postulate

A relation may be proposed as part of the recursive theory.

For example,

\[ \Delta A_{n,\alpha,\mathcal C} = C_{n+1} \lambda_{n+1} \rho_{n+1|n,\alpha}. \]

Physical mapping

A dimensionless R2D quantity may be assigned a conventional physical read.

For example,

\[ S^{\mathrm{th}} = k_B\ln W. \]

Empirical calibration

A specific physical system may quantitatively calibrate a proposed mapping.

Muscle is particularly important in this category.

Hypothesis or projection

Later quantum, gravitational, or cosmological interpretations may remain proposals requiring additional derivation or testing.

Keeping these categories distinct makes the entire program stronger.

A Mapping Is Not a Derivation

Suppose an established physical law can be written exactly in R2D form once a set of physical identifications is supplied.

That is important.

But it is not necessarily a first-principles derivation of the physical law from the Part I axioms.

The distinction is:

\[ \boxed{ \text{R²D relation} + \text{independently justified physical mapping} \Rightarrow \text{known physical law} } \]

versus

\[ \boxed{ \text{R²D axioms alone} \Rightarrow \text{physical law}. } \]

Those are different achievements.

The Canon intentionally keeps them separate.

An Exact Mapping Can Still Be Powerful

Calling something a mapping rather than a derivation does not make it weak.

If independently measured physical quantities occupy exactly the roles predicted by the count architecture, and the mapped relation reproduces an established physical law, that is a nontrivial test.

It means that two descriptions share the same formal structure.

The stronger question—whether every physical parameter can itself be derived from primitive count—can then be asked separately.

Deprojection Runs the Mapping Backward

Once a physical mapping has been established, R2D can also be used in reverse.

Begin with a physical theory:

\[ \text{physical law}. \]

Identify which variables correspond to:

\[ \text{boundary}, \quad \text{multiplicity}, \quad \text{occupancy}, \quad \text{constraint}, \quad \text{recurrence}, \quad \dots \]

Then ask whether the domain-specific ontology can be stripped away while the underlying count relation remains.

This is deprojection.

Schematically:

\[ \boxed{ \text{physical theory} \rightarrow \text{dimensionless count relation}. } \]

That operation is one of the main ways R2D can be used as a scientific discovery tool.

Deprojection Does Not Mean Rejecting the Physical Theory

If a physical law deprojects successfully, its equations need not be wrong.

Quite the opposite.

Their accuracy is evidence that the boundary-specific relation they describe is real.

The question becomes:

What is this equation reading?

rather than

How do we replace this equation?

R2D is therefore often conservative about mathematics while being more radical about ontology.

The Equation Can Survive While the Ontology Changes

This can be written compactly:

\[ \boxed{ \text{same empirical equation} + \text{different primitive interpretation}. } \]

Entropy can remain entropy.

Gibbs free energy can remain Gibbs free energy.

Mechanical work can remain mechanical work.

Quantum probabilities can remain predictive probabilities.

Spacetime curvature can remain physically measurable curvature.

What changes is the claim that any one of these must be primitive before the recursive count relation that makes it meaningful.

Thermodynamics Provides the Calibration Window

The larger program can therefore be pictured as

\[ \boxed{ \text{dimensionless R²D} \rightarrow \text{thermodynamic joint readability} \rightarrow \begin{cases} \text{quantum-facing projection},\\ \text{gravitational-facing projection}. \end{cases} } \]

Thermodynamics is the middle regime where enough of both distinguishability and recurrence remains visible to calibrate the architecture.

The more one side becomes unreadable, the more domain-specific the surviving physical ontology becomes.

The Canon Does Not Assign Equal Confidence to Every Mapping

The current program is deliberately uneven in evidentiary status.

The thermodynamic mappings are the most developed.

Muscle provides unusually direct empirical calibration.

The quantum program contains several structural and mathematical mappings that still require further extension.

The gravitational program identifies recursive geometric structure and multiple physical bridges, but the full derivation connecting the primitive R2D architecture to Einsteinian field geometry remains incomplete.

This is not a defect to hide.

It defines the research program.

What Would Count as Progress

The strongest future R2D results will not be additional verbal analogies.

They will be cases in which:

\[ \boxed{ \text{independently defined count architecture} } \]

predicts

\[ \boxed{ \text{a specific physical mapping} } \]

which then reproduces or extends

\[ \boxed{ \text{an independently established empirical law}. } \]

Better still, the framework should eventually produce new testable relations not inserted through the mapping itself.

That is the transition from reinterpretation to discovery.

What Would Count as Failure

A proposed physical mapping should be rejected if it requires redefining the primitive quantities merely to fit the target equation.

Likewise, the broader R2D program would be challenged if different domains required mutually incompatible definitions of:

  • boundary;
  • multiplicity;
  • possibility;
  • constraint;
  • closure;
  • recurrence;
  • or recursive replacement.

Universality raises the empirical burden.

It does not lower it.

The Same Questions Can Be Asked in Every Domain

For any scientific system, R2D asks:

What boundary defines the object?

What distinctions are readable?

What are the compatible subordinate realizations?

What is the multiplicity?

What occurs?

What occupancy is realized?

What enclosing relation supplies directional possibility?

What constraint supplies retention?

What closes?

What recurs?

What changes meaning when the boundary changes?

Only after answering those questions does it ask:

What physical variables read these relations here?

That is the bridge from count to science.

The Complete Logical Order

The full R2D sequence can now be written:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{boundary} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{distinguishable states} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{multiplicity} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{occurrence and occupancy} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{enclosing possibility and constraint} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{asymmetry} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{closure and recurrence} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{recursive relation} \\[8pt] \Downarrow \\[8pt] \text{physical read} \\[4pt] \Downarrow \\[4pt] \text{thermodynamics, chemistry, mechanics, biology, quantum theory, gravity, \ldots} \end{array} } \]

The physical sciences appear at the end because they assign readable coordinates to an architecture that has already been defined.

The Key Distinction

The central idea is simple:

R2D does not begin by translating one physical theory into another.

It begins beneath their units and objects.

Then it asks how each physical domain reads the same primitive grammar.

Thus:

Entropy is a read of count.

Probability is a normalized read of count.

Work can be a constrained-distinguishability read.

Physical rate can be a recurrence read.

Generated entropy can be a background read.

Geometry can be a distinguishability read.

The physical quantities differ because the boundaries differ.

The proposed invariant is the count architecture beneath them.

Or, most compactly:

The domain changes.
The units change.
The readable ontology changes.
The primitive count grammar does not.
