Constraint and Retention

Possibility can be retained only against a distinguishable change

Possibility tells us which local alternative has greater compatible support from the enclosing count domain.

But possibility alone does not uniquely determine local realization.

A second relation can oppose that directional possibility.

R2D calls that relation constraint.

When constraint acts across a readable local distinction, part of the directional possibility is retained.

The primitive relation is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R = C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda } \]

where

  • \(C^{\mathrm{out}}\) is the productive component of enclosing constraint;
  • \(\lambda\) is the readable distinguishable interval;
  • \(\Delta R\) is local retention.

The distinction is important:

Possibility supplies directional support.
Constraint retains that possibility against distinguishable change.

Possibility →

A Constraint Requires a Distinction

Consider a readable local transition

\[ i \rightarrow k \]

at boundary \(B_n\).

R2D assigns that transition a distinguishable interval

\[ \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

This does not yet mean distance.

It is the boundary-defined size of the readable distinction between the two macrostates.

The reversed transition has opposite orientation:

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_{n,k\rightarrow i} = - \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

Its magnitude,

\[ \left| \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k} \right|, \]

gives the size of the distinction.

No primitive spatial, mechanical, energetic, or temporal interpretation has yet been assigned.

Constraint Comes From the Enclosing Relation

The local transition is defined at \(B_n\).

The relevant constraint is defined by the enclosing count relation at

\[ B_{n+1}. \]

Write the total enclosing constraint as

\[ C_{n+1} \geq 0. \]

This placement is important.

Just as possibility is defined through compatibility with the enclosing boundary, constraint is also an enclosing relation.

Thus the local transition does not contain within itself the complete relation that organizes its realization.

Schematically,

\[ \boxed{ B_{n+1} \longrightarrow \text{constraint on realization at } B_n. } \]

Retention Requires Both Constraint and Distinguishability

For the readable transition

\[ i \rightarrow k, \]

local retention is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

This equation contains two logically distinct quantities.

Constraint

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \]

specifies the productive/output component of the enclosing constraint.

Distinguishability

\[ \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k} \]

specifies the readable local interval against which that constraint acts.

Both are required.

If

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}}=0, \]

then

\[ \Delta R=0 \]

even when a local distinction exists.

Likewise, if

\[ \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}=0, \]

then

\[ \Delta R=0 \]

even when enclosing constraint is present.

Therefore:

Constraint without distinguishability produces no local retention.

and

Distinguishability without productive constraint produces no local retention.

Distinguishability Is Not Created by Constraint

The existence of a readable distinction does not require productive constraint.

Thus,

\[ \boxed{ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}}=0 \quad\not\Rightarrow\quad \lambda_n=0. } \]

A transition can remain distinguishable even when none of its directional possibility is retained as productive output.

This separation becomes important later when the same physical system can possess a readable distinction under zero productive load.

Constraint determines retention of a distinction.

It does not primitively define whether the distinction exists.

Constraint Is Not Yet Force

The notation

\[ C \]

should not be read immediately as mechanical force.

Part I remains dimensionless.

Depending on the physical domain, a later theory may map constrained distinguishability onto quantities involving:

  • force and displacement;
  • chemical work;
  • pressure and volume;
  • field coupling;
  • or some other conjugate physical relation.

But these are later physical calibrations.

The primitive relation is simply

\[ \boxed{ \text{constraint} \times \text{distinguishable interval} = \text{retention}. } \]

This is why R2D can use the same architecture before choosing a specific physical ontology.

Retention Is Oriented

Because

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} > 0 \]

and the distinguishable interval is oriented, retention inherits the orientation of the transition.

Thus,

\[ \Delta R_{n,k\rightarrow i} = - \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

The sign therefore comes from

\[ \lambda. \]

The constraint magnitude need not itself reverse sign.

This lets R2D distinguish:

  • the magnitude of constraint;
  • the magnitude of the distinction;
  • and the orientation of the transition.

That separation later becomes useful when physical quantities such as mechanical direction, charge, or spatial orientation are introduced.

Possibility and Retention Are Different Relations

For a local transition

\[ i \rightarrow k, \]

directional possibility is

\[ \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

Retention is

\[ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

They should not be identified.

Possibility asks:

How differently do the two local alternatives participate in compatible enclosing realizations?

Retention asks:

How much of that directional possibility is retained against the readable local distinction?

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P \neq \Delta R. } \]

Their difference defines the remaining local asymmetry:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A = \Delta P - \Delta R. } \]

Asymmetry →

Three Regimes

The relation

\[ \Delta A = \Delta P-\Delta R \]

immediately gives three important cases.

Incomplete retention

If

\[ \Delta R < \Delta P, \]

then

\[ \Delta A>0. \]

Some directional possibility remains available for local realization.

Complete retention

If

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R=\Delta P, } \]

then

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A=0. } \]

This is the R2D stall condition.

The directional possibility has not disappeared.

It has been completely retained.

Retention greater than directional possibility

If

\[ \Delta R>\Delta P, \]

then

\[ \Delta A<0. \]

The local directional asymmetry reverses.

These regimes are developed more fully in the next section.

Asymmetry →

Zero Retention Is Not Stall

Another important distinction follows immediately.

If

\[ \Delta R=0, \]

then

\[ \Delta A=\Delta P. \]

All of the directional possibility remains locally available.

That is very different from stall.

At stall,

\[ \Delta R=\Delta P \]

and therefore

\[ \Delta A=0. \]

So two apparently quiet or zero-output limits can have opposite meanings.

One can correspond to no productive retention.

The other corresponds to complete retention.

This distinction later becomes directly readable in muscle mechanics.

Why Mechanical Load Is a Useful Example

Muscle provides an especially clear physical calibration.

A myosin transition has a mechanically distinguishable interval.

In a suitable physical mapping,

\[ \lambda_n \longrightarrow d, \]

where \(d\) is a myosin-scale displacement.

The productive constraint acquires a force coordinate,

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \longrightarrow F. \]

Then local retention acquires the energetic read

\[ \Delta R \longrightarrow Fd. \]

This is a physical calibration of the primitive relation

\[ \Delta R = C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda. \]

It does not define \(C\) primitively as force or \(\lambda\) primitively as distance.

The count relation comes first.

The mechanical interpretation comes later.

Changing Constraint Need Not Change Multiplicity

This provides a clean separation among several quantities.

Suppose the native multiplicity landscape remains fixed:

\[ \mathcal{W}_n = \{W_{n,i}\}. \]

Suppose the enclosing possibility relation also remains fixed.

Changing the productive constraint can still change

\[ \Delta R. \]

Therefore it can change

\[ \Delta A. \]

And because asymmetry biases occupancy, the realized state distribution can change.

Thus:

\[ \boxed{ \text{changing constraint} \not\Rightarrow \text{changing native multiplicity}. } \]

A system can move through different realized states of the same multiplicity landscape because its enclosing constraint has changed.

This distinction is particularly useful in systems such as muscle, where mechanical load can be varied experimentally while the molecular state architecture remains present.

Total Constraint and Productive Constraint Are Not the Same

The local retention equation uses only the productive/output component

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}}. \]

But the total enclosing constraint can contain more than one readable component.

R2D writes

\[ \boxed{ C_{n+1} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} + C_{n+1}^{\Phi}. } \]

Here

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \]

is associated with realization that remains readable as distinguishable output.

The term

\[ C_{n+1}^{\Phi} \]

is the background-associated constraint component.

The two act on the same enclosing transition.

They are not two separate physical transitions.

They are not two independent occurrence pathways.

They are two boundary-specific readings associated with one enclosing realization.

Local Retention Uses the Productive Component

At the local boundary,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R_n = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_n. } \]

Only the productive/output component enters local retention.

The background-associated component is not added to \(\Delta R_n\).

This preserves an important distinction:

local retention is not the same quantity as total enclosing constraint.

At the enclosing boundary, however, the full constraint

\[ C_{n+1} \]

enters the recurrence-weighted R2D law.

That law therefore connects two different boundary-specific reads of the same recursively organized relation.

The R²D Law →

Productive and Background-Associated Constraint

The decomposition can be summarized as

\[ \boxed{ C_{n+1} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} + C_{n+1}^{\Phi}. } \]

The productive constrained distinguishability is

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda_{n+1}. \]

The background-associated constrained distinguishability is

\[ C_{n+1}^{\Phi}\lambda_{n+1}. \]

These are distinct reads of the same enclosing occurrence.

The existence of the background-associated component does not mean that background itself is a third directed local coordinate.

The relationship between local nonreciprocal occurrence realization and enclosing background is introduced separately by the R2D Second Law.

The R²D Second Law →

Constraint Does Not “Push” the Component

The equation

\[ \Delta R = C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda \]

can invite a mechanical picture in which constraint physically pushes against a component.

That interpretation is too specific for Part I.

R2D has not yet defined a particle, force, trajectory, or spatial displacement.

The more primitive statement is:

An enclosing constraint acts across a boundary-readable distinction and thereby retains part of the directional possibility associated with that distinction.

A later physical theory can decide how that relation is measured.

The Causal Architecture

Possibility came from enclosing compatibility:

\[ \Theta_{n+1|n,i} \rightarrow P_{n,i} \rightarrow \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

Constraint adds a second enclosing relation:

\[ C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \quad\text{acting through}\quad \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

Together they define local asymmetry:

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

The primitive ordering is therefore

\[ \boxed{ \begin{array}{c} \text{enclosing compatibility} \rightarrow \Delta P \\[4pt] \text{enclosing constraint} \times \text{local distinguishability} \rightarrow \Delta R \\[4pt] \Delta P-\Delta R \rightarrow \Delta A \end{array} } \]

The subordinate transition then realizes occupancy within that organized relation.

The Causal Inversion →

Why Constraint Matters

Constraint makes several distinctions possible that would otherwise be collapsed.

It separates:

  • what is possible from what is retained;
  • a readable distinction from constraint acting on that distinction;
  • directional support from opposition to that support;
  • zero retention from complete retention;
  • and local productive retention from total enclosing constrained realization.

These distinctions allow the same primitive architecture to be mapped later onto thermodynamic, mechanical, chemical, biological, and other physical systems.

Core Equations

Distinguishable interval

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_{n,k\rightarrow i} = - \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

Total enclosing constraint

\[ \boxed{ C_{n+1} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} + C_{n+1}^{\Phi} } \]

Local retention

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} = C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

Net local asymmetry

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} } \]

or

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}} \lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

Complete retention

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P=\Delta R \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta A=0. } \]

The Key Distinction

The simplest summary is:

\(\lambda\) defines the readable difference.

\(C^{\mathrm{out}}\) defines the productive constraint acting across that difference.

\(\Delta R=C^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda\) is the possibility retained against the distinguishable change.

Possibility and retention can now be compared directly.

Their difference is local asymmetry.
